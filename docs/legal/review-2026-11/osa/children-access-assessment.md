## Stage 1 — Is it possible for children to access the service?
- Accounts are for people **18 or over**, by self-declaration at sign-up. Ticket purchase and booking need a payment card.
- Ofcom's guidance: a provider may conclude children **cannot** access the service only if it uses **highly effective age assurance** that prevents access (verify). Self-declaration and payment cards are not highly effective age assurance.
- Public event pages and listings can be viewed without an account.

**Conclusion: it is possible for children to access the service.** → Stage 2.

## Stage 2 — The child user condition
Is there a significant number of UK child users, **or** is the service of a kind likely to attract a significant number of children?

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| Audience and marketing | Marketed to event organisers, venues, suppliers and adult event-goers. No marketing to under-18s **[owner: confirm marketing channels]** |
| Product design | Business tools (planning, ticketing, bookings). Messaging limited to booking/enquiry threads. No public profiles or discovery of private individuals. No feeds, live chat rooms, games or livestream comments |
| Content | Concerts, festivals and nightlife can appeal to 16–17-year-olds; children's parties and school events are organised by adults |
| Payment barrier | Card payment required to buy tickets or book |
| Data | Pre-launch: no usage data yet. **Measure after launch**: under-18 indicators (age prompts, support contacts, ticket age limits, reports with child-safety reason) |

**Provisional conclusion (counsel to confirm): the child user condition is NOT met** — the service is not likely to attract a significant number of children and there is no evidence of significant child use. Some residual risk of 16–17-year-olds signing up to buy concert tickets is recorded and monitored.

## Mitigations kept in place (option 1 in `../online-safety-act.md` §3)
1. 18+ declaration at sign-up; tickets for younger attendees bought by adults only.
2. Messaging limited to booking-linked threads; block and report on messages (Q52); **child safety** report reason → urgent queue (Q74).
3. No public user-to-user discovery of private individuals.
4. Review this assessment after 3 months of live data; earlier if any trigger occurs.

## Triggers that require a new assessment
- Evidence of a significant number of under-18 users (e.g. > [x]% of ticket buyers flagged as under 18, or repeated reports).
- Product changes: consumer public profiles, open messaging, community/forums, livestream chat, teen-targeted events marketing.
- Ofcom guidance changes on "significant number".
